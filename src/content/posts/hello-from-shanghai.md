I have had a GitHub account for twelve years and a folder of half-finished READMEs for almost as long. What I did not have was a place to put the thinking that happens *between* commits: the reason a schema changed, the approach I abandoned, the small decision that turned out to matter six months later.

So I built one. It is intentionally small.

## Why another blog

Most of what I learn while building does not fit in a commit message and does not deserve a full tutorial. It is closer to a field note: here is the problem, here is what I tried, here is what I would do differently. That is the kind of writing I find most useful from other engineers, and it is the kind I can actually keep up with.

The shape of this page is borrowed from the [Cline blog](https://cline.bot/blog) — dark, dense, cards instead of a hero carousel. I liked that it treats writing as a body of work rather than a marketing funnel. There is no newsletter popup here, no cookie banner, no analytics script. Just pages, an RSS feed, and whatever I manage to publish.

## What lives here

Three things, roughly:

- **Writing** — build logs and product notes. How a feature was actually built, what broke, what I would keep.
- **Projects** — the products I ship outside of work: Porkast, Trend Catcher, MovTrak and JD Matcher. Each has a page with the stack, the current status and a link to the live thing.
- **About** — the short version of who I am, what I care about, and how to reach me.

There is no publishing schedule. The RSS feed is the honest way to follow along; if a month goes by without a post, that is probably because something in one of those products was on fire.

## The short version of me

I work on infrastructure at eBay. It is a job that rewards patience, a tolerance for ambiguity, and a certain affection for systems that quietly do their job. It has also shaped how I build my own products: keep the surface small, keep the dependencies boring, and ship the simplest version that survives contact with reality.

Outside of work I build for the web, for iOS, and increasingly for AI agents. Porkast turns podcast subscriptions into keyword feeds and personal RSS. Trend Catcher reads the internet every morning so indie developers do not have to. MovTrak turns an iPhone into a camera that follows a climber. JD Matcher reads job listings against a resume and vetoes the ones that waste your time.

None of those are platforms. They are tools for a specific person — often me — and that is exactly why I like them.

## Say hello

If you are building something in the same neighbourhood — infrastructure, small products, agent tooling — I would like to hear about it. Email is the most reliable way to reach me, and GitHub is where most of the code lives.

Thanks for reading. More build logs are already in the drafts folder.
